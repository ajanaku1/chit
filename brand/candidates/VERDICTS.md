# Chit logo candidate verdicts

## A — Occluded fold · Grade B

- **Concept fit:** The visible endpoints and hidden middle express private attribution directly.
- **Brand compliance:** The single accent and paper neutrals stay inside the approved constraints.
- **Craft:** The asymmetric paper ends and central fold form one clean object.
- **Legibility:** It is the fastest candidate to recognize at 16px.
- **Ownability:** The fold can recur in dividers and transitions, though the silhouette risks reading as a transfer icon.
- **Verdict:** The idea is right; the transfer-icon resemblance is an execution-level risk.

## B — Blind seam · Grade B

- **Concept fit:** A physical chit divided by an unreadable seam ties the product name to its privacy mechanism.
- **Brand compliance:** It uses one accent, no security cliché, and no generic crypto geometry.
- **Craft:** The paired notches make the seam deliberate rather than a decorative crack.
- **Legibility:** The split remains visible at 16px without turning into noise.
- **Ownability:** The blind seam is the strongest candidate for a repeatable product motif.
- **Verdict:** Best concept overall; the small-size hourglass resemblance is an execution-level refinement.

## C — Quiet pool · Grade B

- **Concept fit:** Three inputs disappear at one privacy band and resolve into a pooled public total.
- **Brand compliance:** It avoids coins, locks, shields, nodes, and token-shaped enclosures.
- **Craft:** The strict vertical rhythm is clear, though more abstract than A or B.
- **Legibility:** The three inputs, privacy band, and output notch remain distinct at 16px.
- **Ownability:** The masked pool can become a settlement diagram motif, but needs context to explain itself.
- **Verdict:** Structurally sound and distinct; its abstraction is the tradeoff rather than a rendering flaw.

## Recommendation

Choose **B — Blind seam**. Its idea is the most specific to Chit, and its remaining issue can be
resolved in the selected-direction refinement rather than by changing the concept.
