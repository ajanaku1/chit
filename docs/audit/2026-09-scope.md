# Chit: audit scope

Prepared 2026-09-19 for the firm audit before full mainnet. Contact: Lucian
(@algo_cats), advisor; the developer is Bambam (ajanaku1 on GitHub). The
repository is private; access is by invite.

## What Chit is, in the words the audit should hold us to

Chit is a private funding layer for trading fleets on Robinhood Chain. A
trader holds a balance with Chit; Chit funds the trader's fleet of fresh
wallets and pays their gas, so no transaction joins the trader's main wallet
to a fleet account. The claim is bounded on purpose (spec FR-015): the main
wallet never funds the fleet; the chain shows a deposit into Chit and fleets
funded by Chit with no transaction linking them; **the operator can link
them**; amounts and timing may be guessed at while the pool is small. Chit is
private, not anonymous, and never says otherwise. The threat model below
follows from that sentence.

## Chain and dependencies

- Robinhood Chain, an Arbitrum Orbit chain. Mainnet id 4663, testnet 46630.
- ERC-4337 EntryPoint v0.7 (`0x0000000071727De22E5E9d8BAf0edAc6f37da032`).
- Uniswap v4: PoolManager, Universal Router (`execute(bytes,bytes[],uint256)`),
  Permit2. Swaps are exact-in ETH → token and token → ETH through the router.
- OpenZeppelin Contracts (Ownable, Ownable2Step, Math). Solidity 0.8.28 (0.8.24
  for the buyback). Hardhat; tests in Solidity (forge-style `.t.sol`) and
  TypeScript, including fork tests against the live router.

## Scope B: session keys (branch `feat/session-keys`)

| Contract | Lines | Code | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `contracts/fleet/SessionAccount.sol` | 235 | 175 | An owner's account that executes calls for a granted key within rules `(target, selector)`, a per-call value cap, a total cap and an expiry; pause, resume, revoke; owner-only withdraw of ETH and tokens. |
| `SessionAccount.sol`, the sell flag (branch `feat/session-sell-flag`) | +73 | +40 | Per key, owner-set `sellAllowed`. With it a live key calls `approveForSell(token, spender)`: the account approves Permit2 (`0x0000…78BA3`, constant) on `token` without limit and Permit2 approves `spender` without limit, `spender` required to be a target of that key's rules. Nothing else changes: the sale is a normal `execute` with zero value. |
| `contracts/fleet/SessionAccountFactory.sol` | 36 | 22 | One account per owner, address known before creation. No owner, no operator. |

This is the primitive under the non-custodial trading bot: the bot signs
`execute` with its own key, the account pays the trade, the owner revokes in
one transaction. The sell flag is the one addition `docs/bot-mainnet-mode.md`
named, now built and in scope. The question for the audit is exactly this:
given an unlimited token approval to Permit2 and an unlimited Permit2
allowance to a rule target, can any party other than the account itself,
calling that target under its own session, move the account's tokens? Our
reading of Permit2 and the Universal Router says no (Permit2 transfers only
for `spender` as `msg.sender`, and the router pulls only from its caller);
we want that read confirmed, and any token whose `approve` misbehaves named.

## Trust model

- **Operator** (one key, `0x34b0…F724` on testnet; a different key on
  mainnet): deploys, executes sponsored buys through the paymaster, settles
  gas against the escrow, draws from the pool to fleets it created. The pool
  operator is immutable in the pool. The operator can link a depositor to a
  fleet off chain; this is stated, not hidden.
- **Guardian** (mainnet, required): a second key that can only `pause()` the
  pool.
- **Depositor / trader**: deposits fixed sizes into the pool from their own
  wallet; can self-exit after 24 h; owns the fleet accounts' keys (generated
  in the browser, sealed into a recovery vault the service never opens).
- **Fleet account**: an ERC-4337 account owned by the trader's key, trading
  under the session policy with gas from the paymaster.
- **Buyback**: nobody. Anyone can fund it and anyone can call it; the
  parameters are immutable.
- **Session account (B)**: the owner; a granted key can only call what the
  rules allow with what the caps allow, and can never transfer out.

## Invariants we believe hold and want checked

1. The pool never draws more than its per-draw cap to one fleet, never
   holds more than its cap, never lets a depositor exceed the per-depositor
   cap, and lets a depositor exit their own unspent balance without the
   operator.
2. No path moves ETH from the pool anywhere but to a fleet the operator
   created for that draw, or back to the depositor on exit.
3. The paymaster sponsors only accounts under a live policy and never more
   than the policy's per-account gas; the escrow settles only what the
   paymaster spent.
4. A fleet account cannot be made to trade outside its policy (router,
   function, max per trade, expiry) by anyone, the operator included.
5. `buyAndBurn` cannot be called more than once per interval, cannot spend
   outside `[floor, cap]` or more than 1% of the balance, cannot receive a
   fill more than 5% under the pool's own quote, and leaves no CHIT in the
   contract after any call. There is no function that moves ETH out except
   the swap.
6. (B) A session key can never exceed its per-call or total caps, call a
   `(target, selector)` outside its rules, act while paused, after expiry or
   after revoke, or move tokens or ETH out of the account.
7. (B) `approveForSell` succeeds only for a key that exists, is not paused,
   revoked or expired, has `sellAllowed` set by the owner, and names a
   spender that is a target of its rules; it touches no cap and no counter,
   and the only Permit2 spender it can ever create an allowance for is one
   the key was already allowed to call.

## Threat model, the questions we want answered

- Can the operator, alone or with a colluding depositor, take pool ETH that
  is not theirs?
- Can a depositor drain or lock the pool for others (griefing the delay,
  exit, or pause paths)?
- Can anyone front-run or sandwich `buyAndBurn` past the 5% guard, or grief
  the interval so the buyback stalls?
- Reentrancy on every ETH-moving path (pool deposit/exit/draw, escrow
  settlement, buyback swap, session execute).
- ERC-4337 specifics: paymaster validation, signature aggregation, replay
  across chains (the same bytecode runs on 46630 and 4663).
- Deterministic addresses: can an attacker pre-deploy or squat a fleet or
  session account address?
- Anything that would make the FR-015 claim false: a transaction that links
  a depositor's main wallet to a fleet account on chain.

## Out of scope

The hosted service (TypeScript on Vercel), the web app, the Telegram bot,
the landing site, the keeper scripts and the Telegram posts. They are
reviewed separately (`docs/audit/2026-09-16-chit-bot.md`). The audit is of
the contracts and their interaction with EntryPoint, the router and Permit2.

## What we ask for

- A report with findings by severity, each with a reproduction and a
  recommended fix; a re-check of the fixes.
- A named commit for the audited state, and the mainnet bytecode verified
  against it after deployment.
- The audit's own words on the FR-015 claim: what the chain shows and what
  it does not, so the marketing can quote the auditor rather than itself.
